## Executive Summary

Across ten sources, resilience for transient failures is nearly universal — every source implements exponential backoff somewhere — but the three sub-capabilities of this dimension split sharply along product-shape lines. **Retry** is mature where a single team owns the execution layer (temporal's pluggable policy hierarchy, langgraph's engine-level `RetryPolicy`, openai-agents-sdk's policy-callable runner, langfuse's two-tier queue design) and ad-hoc where it accreted per subsystem (crewai's ~10 independent loops, letta's ~8 hand-rolled implementations, agent-framework's fragmented knob vocabulary). **Fallback** is effectively absent as an automated mechanism in 9 of 10 sources: only pydantic-ai ships a first-class `FallbackModel` provider chain (pydantic_ai_slim/pydantic_ai/models/fallback.py:104-133), and only letta has the architecture without the implementation (its Redis-backed routing client is missing from the OSS tree, letta/services/llm_router/llm_router_client_base.py:14-97). **Degraded mode** is the strongest dimension overall, but it lives at the infrastructure tier (opa serves last-known-good policies through outages; temporal converts overload into `ResourceExhausted`; langfuse reroutes noisy projects to secondary queues; letta removes degraded pods from rotation) rather than at the LLM/model tier.

The dimension's guiding question — *can the system survive a provider outage without failing all requests?* — gets opposite answers depending on which "provider" is meant. For infrastructure providers (bundle services, databases, queues), opa, temporal, and langfuse answer yes with concrete mechanisms. For model providers, the answer across agent frameworks is no: a sustained outage of the configured LLM exhausts local retries and fails runs, unless the user wires pydantic-ai's fallback chain or has letta's enterprise router. Circuit breakers exist in only 3 of 10 sources (temporal's gobreaker pool, langfuse's two TTL-keyed Redis breakers, openhands' coarse persisted disable flag); letta interfaces one but doesn't ship it.

The most transferable finding is what separates the 7+ scores from the 5–6 scores: not more retry code, but (1) centralized error classification driving policy selection, (2) explicit ownership handoff to prevent multiplicative hidden retries, (3) replay/idempotency safety boundaries around side-effecting operations, and (4) tests that pin exact backoff timing and exhaustion semantics.

## Core Thesis

Retry/fallback/degradation maturity correlates less with effort than with **where ownership of failure is placed**. Sources that designate one seam that owns failure — langgraph's Pregel runner wrapping every task (`run_with_retry`), temporal's decorator clients at each trust boundary, openai-agents-sdk's runner-managed retry loops with a provider-advice protocol, pydantic-ai's five explicitly budgeted layers — score high because behavior is uniform, auditable, and testable at the seam. Sources that distribute failure handling across call sites — crewai, letta, agent-framework's chat path, openhands' frontend — accumulate divergent vocabularies (`progress_ledger_retry_count` vs `retry_attempts` vs `ClientRetryPolicy(n)` in agent-framework; eight bespoke backoff loops in letta), jitter-free or delay-free hot paths, and untestable worst-case latency. Meanwhile, automatic provider failover is a market gap across the entire cohort: even the highest-scoring sources punt on it (openai-agents-sdk explicitly routes by prefix at resolution time only, multi_provider.py:199-225; langfuse surfaces terminal `ERROR` statuses rather than switching models). The practical consequence for anyone building on these systems: assume you own the last line of outage defense, and copy the *seams* these projects expose rather than their defaults.

## Approach Models

Clustering the ten sources by how they answer "who owns retry?" yields four approach models:

**Model A — Engine-owned declarative policy (langgraph, openai-agents-sdk, temporal).** Retry is a named, public configuration type evaluated by a single execution seam. LangGraph defines `RetryPolicy(NamedTuple)` with interval/factor/cap/attempts/jitter/retry_on fields applied inside the Pregel runner (libs/langgraph/langgraph/types.py:418-437; libs/langgraph/langgraph/pregel/_retry.py:573-617), configurable per node, graph-wide, or via the functional API with documented precedence (libs/langgraph/langgraph/graph/state.py:272-330). The OpenAI Agents SDK instead makes policy *code*: composable `RetryPolicy` callables receiving rich context (error, attempt, provider advice) return a `RetryDecision` (src/agents/retry.py:142-185; src/agents/retry.py:304-462). Temporal generalizes the idea across trust boundaries with a `backoff.RetryPolicy` interface and six policy variants including conditional and resource-exhaustion-aware throttle modes (common/backoff/retrypolicy.go:35-44; common/backoff/retry.go:15-102).

**Model B — Composition over convention (agent-framework, pydantic-ai transports, crewai SDK layer).** The core ships no retry dependency; users assemble resilience from extension points plus libraries. Agent-framework's designated seam is chat/agent middleware, with tenacity wired up in a sample rather than the framework (python/packages/core/agent_framework/_middleware.py:546-566; python/samples/02-agents/auto_retry.py:87-93); Pydantic AI similarly requires installing a `[retries]` extra and attaching a tenacity transport yourself (docs/models/http-request-retries.md:19; pydantic_ai_slim/pydantic_ai/retries.py:72-134). This keeps cores dependency-light but makes outage behavior a per-application property.

**Model C — Infrastructure-tier durability (langfuse, temporal, letta ops side).** Where work is enqueued or scheduled, retries become job/timer semantics rather than call-site loops. Langfuse layers declarative BullMQ `attempts`+`backoff` under application-level age-budgeted re-enqueues carrying persisted `RetryBaggage` (packages/shared/src/server/redis/ingestionQueue.ts:64-72; worker/src/features/utils/retry-handler.ts:106-258; packages/shared/src/server/queues.ts:388-393). Temporal materializes user-facing activity/workflow retries as durable timer tasks with attempt counts in workflow mutable state, surviving restarts and shard failover (service/history/workflow/task_generator.go:572-583; service/history/workflow/mutable_state_impl.go:6930-6999).

**Model D — Ad-hoc per-subsystem loops (crewai, letta, opa's consumers, openhands).** Each subsystem reimplements roughly the same loop. CrewAI's `2**attempt` formula appears independently in five places with no jitter (lib/crewai/src/crewai/mcp/client.py:714 among others); letta has at least eight implementations with divergent knobs, from a 429-only decorator hardcoding `max_retries=20` (letta/llm_api/llm_api_tools.py:43) to a curated `_RETRYABLE_ERRORS` tuple in its ChatGPT client (letta/llm_api/chatgpt_oauth_client.py:100-102). OPA is the partial exception worth noting: the *math* is shared (`util.DefaultBackoff`, v1/util/backoff.go:12-43) while scheduling loops are bespoke per consumer (v1/download/download.go:215-262; v1/plugins/logs/plugin.go:902-961).

These models predict scores well: Model A sources average 8/10, Model C 7.7/10 (dragged down only by letta's absent router), Models B/D average 6/10.

## Pattern Catalog

### 1. Ownership handoff to prevent multiplicative retries

- **Problem:** layered systems naturally double- or triple-retry (SDK × middleware × loop), inflating latency during exactly the incidents when it hurts most.
- **Demonstrated by:** openai-agents-sdk disables provider-managed retries via a ContextVar + `with_options(max_retries=0)` precisely when the runner takes over scheduling, decided by a centralized predicate matrix (src/agents/models/_retry_runtime.py:138-158; src/agents/run_internal/model_retry.py:516-554). CrewAI bypasses agent-level retries for any exception whose module starts with `"litellm"` (lib/crewai/src/crewai/agent/core.py:140-141 region, litellm-module check). Agent-framework shows the failure mode it prevents: Python OpenAI wrappers never pass `max_retries`, so user middleware silently wraps implicit SDK retries into multiplied attempt counts (python/packages/openai/agent_framework_openai/_shared.py:235-246).
- **Why it works:** exactly one layer schedules attempts at any moment; the others become inert.
- **Copy when:** you have both transport-level and semantic-level retry anywhere in the stack.
- **Risky/overkill when:** the coordination matrix becomes cognitive overhead — the OpenAI Agents SDK report flags its decision matrix (attempt × statefulness × policy presence) as heavily tested but expensive to modify.
- **Evidence:** openai-agents-sdk (src/agents/run_internal/model_retry.py:516-554), crewai (agent/core.py litellm bypass), agent-framework (_shared.py:235-246).

### 2. Replay/idempotency safety boundaries

- **Problem:** retrying an operation whose outcome is unknown can double-execute side effects (charged calls, sent messages, tool mutations).
- **Demonstrated by:** openai-agents-sdk implements three independent fail-closed vetoes — aborts, already-emitted stream output, and stateful (`previous_response_id`/`conversation_id`) requests — liftable only by explicit `approve_unsafe_replay` or provider-marked-safe advice (src/agents/run_internal/model_retry.py:419-479; src/agents/retry.py:124-129). Agent-framework's MCP layer refuses blind re-sends before a `task_id` exists ("cannot safely retry") and allows exactly one reconnect-retry afterward (python/packages/core/agent_framework/_mcp.py:2372-2381; :2509-2546). Letta's streaming client refuses retry once chunks have been yielded (letta/llm_api/chatgpt_oauth_client.py:626-705 region). Pydantic AI pins suspended-stream continuations to their serving model, cancels orphaned server-side jobs, rewinds history, and stamps a `replace_previous_response` marker on chain re-entry (pydantic_ai_slim/pydantic_ai/models/fallback.py:253-280).
- **Why it works:** it trades availability for exactly-once semantics *per operation class*, rather than globally.
- **Copy when:** any retried operation writes, charges, or emits externally visible output.
- **Contrast:** openhands is the cautionary absence — its `withRetry` wraps secret/settings PUTs with no dedupe keys, so a server-side success followed by a client timeout could double-apply (src/api/with-retry.ts:4-26; src/api/secrets-service.ts:69-136).
- **Evidence:** openai-agents-sdk, agent-framework, letta, pydantic-ai; gap documented in openhands.

### 3. Server-honored pacing (Retry-After / provider advice)

- **Problem:** computed backoff ignores what the server actually asked for, prolonging rate-limit recovery and wasting quota.
- **Demonstrated by:** pydantic-ai parses `Retry-After` in seconds *and* HTTP-date form with caps in its wait strategy and exception surface (pydantic_ai_slim/pydantic_ai/retries.py:514-588; exceptions.py:534-540); openai-agents-sdk parses `retry-after-ms`/`retry-after` anywhere in the error chain and gives explicit policy delay → provider `retry_after` → computed backoff precedence (src/agents/models/_retry_runtime.py:60-97; src/agents/run_internal/model_retry.py:481-491). CrewAI honors `Retry-After` in its Brave Search tool and mirrors it as `RateLimitExceededError.retry_after` in A2A — but notably *not* in its LLM layer (lib/crewai-tools/src/crewai_tools/tools/brave_search_tool/base.py:67-79).
- **Why it works:** the server knows its capacity; honoring it converts adversarial hammering into cooperative pacing.
- **Copy when:** you talk to anything that emits the header (most LLM gateways do).
- **Gap elsewhere:** langgraph's engine knows only exceptions and time — it cannot honor `Retry-After` because retry belongs to the execution layer, a deliberate trade documented in its report (libs/langgraph/langgraph/pregel/_runner.py wiring; `_internal/_retry.py:1-29` classification).
- **Evidence:** pydantic-ai, openai-agents-sdk, crewai; absence noted in langgraph.

### 4. TTL-keyed, self-healing circuit breakers

- **Problem:** sustained outages shouldn't cost every caller full retry budgets; something must remember "this target is down."
- **Demonstrated by:** langfuse implements two breaker-like mechanisms without naming them: an S3-SlowDown per-project Redis TTL flag that redirects ingestion to an isolated secondary queue and expires closed automatically (packages/shared/src/server/redis/s3SlowdownTracking.ts:39-74; worker/src/queues/ingestionQueue.ts:111-136), and a webhook auto-disable after ≥5 consecutive failures via a 24h-TTL counter reset on success (worker/src/queues/webhooks.ts:56-75; :428-465). Temporal runs a gobreaker two-step breaker pool keyed by namespace/task-group/destination whose open state converts failures into `ResourceExhausted(CIRCUIT_BREAKER_OPEN)` so tasks back off instead of cascading or hitting the DLQ (common/circuitbreaker/circuitbreaker.go:19-71; service/history/circuitbreakerpool/fx.go:20-76; service/history/queues/executable.go:951-963). Openhands persists a coarse variant: five consecutive failed probes mark a backend disabled in localStorage until a human intervenes (src/api/backend-registry/health-storage.ts:5-10; src/hooks/query/use-backends-health.ts:241-283).
- **Why it works:** TTL expiry gives automatic half-open-ish recovery without background closers; converting breaker-open into a protocol error (`ResourceExhausted`) lets downstream SDKs participate.
- **Copy when:** targets have distinct blast radii (per project/destination/backend key).
- **Risky when:** recovery needs external stimulus — openhands' disabled verdict survives reloads and re-arms only via config edits or manual probes, risking a healthy backend staying marked dead.
- **Evidence:** langfuse, temporal, openhands; letta interfaces the same signals (`record_failure`/`record_success`) but the implementing module is absent (letta/services/llm_router/llm_router_client_base.py:42-46).

### 5. Last-known-good serving and fail-open/fail-closed discipline

- **Problem:** upstream config/data delivery failing should never take down the serving path.
- **Demonstrated by:** OPA keeps the previously activated bundle serving traffic through download/activation failures, pinned by test (v1/plugins/bundle/plugin.go:513-544; plugin_test.go:3985-4000), and classifies telemetry as best-effort — bounded FIFO buffers drop oldest-first with named counters rather than blocking decisions (v1/plugins/logs/buffer.go:11-57; drop counters at v1/plugins/logs/plugin.go:274-277). Langfuse makes the philosophy explicit per path: fail open where availability beats strictness (rate limiter errors ignored so ingestion continues, web/src/pages/api/public/ingestion.ts:124-131) versus fail closed for data integrity (S3 upload failure aborts the batch, processEventBatch.ts:328-332). Agent-framework's .NET Foundry hosting maps degraded startup states to Healthy and defers toolbox enumeration to per-request retries, keeping the container routable (dotnet FoundryToolboxStartupStatus.cs:39-64; FoundryToolboxService.cs:334-404).
- **Why it works:** availability of the primary function is decoupled from freshness/telemetry delivery.
- **Copy when:** your product has a clear "primary function" that stale data can still serve.
- **Cost:** silent degradation can mask bugs — CrewAI warns about WARN-by-default tool failures producing lower-quality outputs without hard signals.
- **Evidence:** opa, langfuse, agent-framework (.NET hosting).

### 6. Degrade-by-transforming-the-request

- **Problem:** some failures are caused by the payload itself (too big, unsupported params, context overflow); sleeping and resending identically cannot help.
- **Demonstrated by:** langfuse's ClickHouse writer splits batches in half on string-length errors and truncates oversized fields with a marker, using the retry callback as a repair hook (worker/src/services/ClickhouseWriter/index.ts:406-498; truncation at :216-286). Letta shrinks embedding batches adaptively until they succeed (letta/llm_api/openai_client.py:1085-1203). CrewAI drops unsupported parameters and retries once, summarizes messages on context overflow by default, and falls back from Responses API to chat-completions on endpoint 404s (lib/crewai/src/crewai/llm.py:1909-1946; utilities/agent_utils.py:795-832; llms/providers/openai/completion.py:497-564). Letta compacts message history on `ContextWindowExceededError` then re-requests within the same bounded loop (letta/agents/letta_agent_v3.py:1218-1263).
- **Why it works:** it converts deterministic failures into solvable ones instead of burning budget.
- **Copy when:** error classes correlate with request shape.
- **Risk:** mutation between attempts must stay observable, or quality degrades invisibly (CrewAI's drop-param self-healing is flagged for this).
- **Evidence:** langfuse, letta, crewai.

### 7. Semantic retries as conversation, bounded by budgets

- **Problem:** model output errors (invalid JSON, failed validation, wrong tool signature) need the model to change behavior, not the scheduler to repeat the call.
- **Demonstrated by:** pydantic-ai turns `ModelRetry` exceptions into `RetryPromptPart` messages appended to history, consumed against per-tool/per-output budgets that raise actionable errors when exhausted (pydantic_ai_slim/pydantic_ai/messages.py:1637-1721; _agent_graph.py:361-378; tool_manager.py:256-265). Agent-framework's group-chat selector feeds the parse-failure text back as a corrective instruction (python/packages/orchestrations/agent_framework_orchestrations/_group_chat.py:537-542); its Magentic orchestrators use cheap linear micro-backoff tuned for nondeterministic-generation errors rather than network congestion (_magentic.py:719-739). Gemini retries in letta append corrective text about special characters after `MALFORMED_FUNCTION_CALL` (letta/llm_api/google_vertex_client.py:186-191).
- **Why it works:** the model, not a blind loop, decides what to fix; budgets keep it terminating.
- **Copy when:** failures are validation/parse-shaped. Use plain transport backoff for network-shaped ones.
- **Hygiene detail worth copying:** pydantic-ai resets per-tool counters on success so alternating fail/succeed patterns don't starve small budgets (tool_manager.py:187-220).
- **Evidence:** pydantic-ai, agent-framework, letta.

### 8. Terminal quarantine (DLQ) with replay tooling

- **Problem:** poison messages must exit the retry path without being lost.
- **Demonstrated by:** Temporal routes terminally failed tasks to a DLQ governed by dynamic config with regex-matched patterns and admin tooling (service/history/queues/executable.go:80-91; :543-640). Langfuse retains 100k failed jobs via `removeOnFail` and replays five queues' dead letters every ~10 minutes via a histogram-instrumented cron (packages/shared/src/server/redis/ingestionQueue.ts:64-72; worker/src/services/dlq/dlqRetryService.ts:9-63). OPA's inverse pattern — requeue-not-drop within a bounded buffer (docs/docs/management-decision-logs.md:86-89) — covers the same need pre-quarantine.
- **Evidence:** temporal, langfuse, opa. Notably absent from all agent frameworks in this study, which simply raise to callers.

### 9. Retry observability as a first-class contract

- **Demonstrated by:** langfuse emits distributions for retry count and cumulative delay on every retry path plus span events per attempt (worker/src/features/utils/retry-handler.ts:195-213); temporal records typed `RetryState` outcomes (`MAXIMUM_ATTEMPTS_REACHED`, `NON_RETRYABLE_FAILURE`, …) into history events, making termination reasons user-visible, not just logs (service/history/workflow/retry.go:95-111); letta decorates loops with OTEL events like `llm_retry_attempt` (letta/llm_api/llm_api_tools.py:72-93). Weakest in cohort: langgraph logs + `node_attempt` only; openai-agents-sdk debug logs plus synthetic zero-token usage entries; openhands nothing dedicated.
- **Copy:** metrics-on-every-branch (langfuse) + typed outcome enums (temporal) is the strongest combination observed.
- **Evidence:** langfuse, temporal, letta vs. langgraph, openai-agents-sdk, openhands.

### 10. Exact-timing and negative-path tests as the maturity marker

Every 7+ source pins retry behavior with tests asserting precise schedules and exhaustion: langgraph's ~2,900-line suite mocks `time.sleep`/`random.uniform` to assert exact sequences (libs/langgraph/tests/test_retry.py:249-306) and proves fresh-budget re-execution after restart (tests/test_pregel.py:978); openai-agents-sdk's 3,714-line model-retry suite covers vetoes, coordination, cancellation, and streaming windows; temporal asserts DataLoss retries exactly N times (common/persistence/persistence_retryable_clients_test.go:19-88); crewai proves retried attempts open/close their own event scopes (lib/crewai/tests/utilities/test_events.py:376-469). Conversely, the 5–6 sources systematically lack dedicated tests for their most-used loops (letta: embeddings retry only; openhands: no `withRetry` tests; agent-framework: no Magentic ledger-retry tests).

## Key Differences

**Where the studied behavior lives differs more than what it does.** Agent frameworks concentrate retry at either the runner/engine layer (langgraph Pregel runner, openai-agents-sdk run loop) or leave it to users entirely (agent-framework chat path, crewai agent loops with immediate re-entry). Platform products concentrate it at infrastructure boundaries (langfuse queues, temporal persistence/service decorators, opa plugin loops). Openhands, as a frontend, deliberately owns none of the LLM tier — a dead `STATUS$LLM_RETRY` status literal is the residue of a dropped contract (src/types/runtime-status.ts:18) — and instead invests in connection survival and recovery UX.

**Fallback models/providers: the starkest divergence in the study.** Only pydantic-ai treats fallback as a first-class `Model` wrapper — sequential chain, customizable `fallback_on` triggers (exception predicates or response-content handlers), cost accounting of rejected responses folded into the winner, OTel spans refreshed to whichever inner model actually answered (models/fallback.py:104-133; :296-307; :477-489). Letta architected the same capability (step-loop fallback switching, billing-aware model resolution, breaker signals) but its Redis-backed router is absent from OSS and silently substituted by a noop stub (letta/services/llm_router/__init__.py:6-12; llm_router_client_base.py:38-93). Everyone else: absent. Notably, langfuse's report observes fallback could hide behind a user-configured gateway baseURL, and openai-agents-sdk registers multiple providers yet never switches on failure — prefix routing is resolution-time only (multi_provider.py:199-225). This is a product decision, not a capability ceiling: library authors push failover to composition time; platform owners assume the gateway/load-balancer handles it.

**Circuit breakers: present only where someone owns steady-state operations.** Temporal (destination-scoped gobreaker pool) and langfuse (project-scoped TTL flags) operate fleets and feel the cascade pain directly. All six pure libraries lack them; several compensate differently (pydantic-ai's `UsageLimits.request_limit=50` bounds runaway loops per run; langgraph cancels sibling tasks on failure as a super-step abort; crewai relies on per-call guards that "each pay full retry cost against dead endpoints").

**Degradation targets differ by layer.** Libraries degrade *semantics* (crewai summarizes overflowing context; pydantic-ai synthesizes responses via capability hooks; openai-agents-sdk's `RunErrorHandlers` substitute validated final outputs). Platforms degrade *scheduling* (opa serves old bundles; letta flips `/ready` to 503 and drains; langfuse sheds load deterministically and returns HTTP 207 partial success). These compose rather than compete: letta's pod removal plus crewai-style summarization would be complementary in one system.

**Persistence philosophy converges on "persist outcomes, keep counters volatile" — with temporal as the deliberate exception.** LangGraph commits `(ERROR, exception)` pending writes so failed tasks route to handlers across restarts, but re-executes them with fresh attempt budgets (libs/langgraph/langgraph/pregel/_loop.py:736-749; :751-816). OpenAI Agents SDK excludes the policy callable from serialization and scopes counters to one invocation by design (src/agents/retry.py:219-220). Temporal alone persists attempt counts and next-retry timers durably because it is the system of record (mutable_state_impl.go:6930-6999). The middle cases expose the cost of volatility: langfuse's ClickHouse writer resets counters on restart and drops rows at exhaustion with only metrics as trace (ClickhouseWriter/index.ts:532-572, acknowledged TODO at :544); letta's crashed processes restart with fresh retry budgets.

## Tradeoffs

**Composition vs. built-in defaults.** Agent-framework and pydantic-ai keep cores slim and flexible; the bill is that "two applications of the same framework can have wildly different outage behavior" (agent-framework) and out-of-the-box flakiness fails runs (pydantic-ai docs: nothing retries below the agent loop unless you install a retrying transport, docs/models/http-request-retries.md:19). Langfuse/temporal invert this: declarative queue options and decorator clients mean default survivability, at the cost of more machinery to understand. Best-fit: libraries favor composition *if* they document a canonical recipe loudly (the openai-agents-sdk report recommends promoting `examples/basic/retry.py`'s baseline policy for exactly this reason).

**Fail-closed replay safety vs. availability.** OpenAI Agents SDK fails a transient blip on a stateful follow-up unless the app grants approval; agent-framework refuses ambiguous MCP re-sends even though a retry would usually be safe for read-only tools ("no per-tool idempotency opt-in exists"). LangGraph takes the opposite pole: `default_retry_on` retries unknown exceptions by default (libs/langgraph/langgraph/_internal/_retry.py:29), maximizing survival but risking non-idempotent double-execution. There is no consensus; the correct choice depends entirely on operation cost asymmetry.

**Durable retries vs. latency.** Temporal's durable timers add a DB write per retry-scheduling decision; its in-memory fast-resubmit path exists to skip that cost for benign errors, accepting loss of those attempts on restart (executable.go:795-851). Every other source implicitly made the opposite choice (volatile counters) without documenting the trade.

**Indefinite infra retry vs. termination guarantees.** OPA's eternal download loops guarantee eventual convergence but "can mask a permanently broken endpoint" behind WARN states; langfuse reconnects Redis forever with capped delays. Application-tier sources universally prefer bounded budgets. The split tracks whether failure detection is delegated to humans watching dashboards or must surface to end users.

**Isolation vs. throttling under overload.** Langfuse routes misbehaving projects to sharded secondary queues — containing blast radius without rejecting traffic — at the price of duplicated queue definitions and redirect plumbing everywhere. Alternatives seen: letta removes pods from load-balancer rotation (coarse but simple); pydantic-ai raises `ConcurrencyLimitExceeded` past queue depth caps (reject-fast); temporal normalizes everything to `ResourceExhausted`.

**Silent stubs vs. loud failure.** Letta's conditional import substitutes a noop router so OSS builds stay green — but hides missing capability until a `RuntimeError` mid-step, and its noop Redis client quietly disables duplicate-request recovery that client retries depend on (letta/data_sources/redis_client.py:663-681). Compare openai-agents-sdk's approach of making everything opt-in and documented instead.

**Feature-gated protection default-off.** Both letta (all four load gates off, letta/settings.py:600-637) and langfuse (S3 slowdown breaker `LANGFUSE_S3_RATE_ERROR_SLOWDOWN_ENABLED` default false) shipped working safeguards disabled by default. Stock deployments therefore degrade by saturating (letta) or lose the isolation safeguard (langfuse) — backward compatibility purchased with operational risk.

## Decision Guide

**If you build a library/SDK** (callers own deployment):
1. Adopt pydantic-ai's five-layer separation with independent budgets — it names transport, fallback, tool, output, and hook retries and states plainly that they don't share budgets (docs/retries.md:5-15). This structurally prevents multiplicative-retry bugs.
2. Model fallback as a wrapper implementing your model/client interface (pydantic-ai's `FallbackModel`), not as an agent feature — it stays orthogonal, stackable, and usable outside agents.
3. If you manage provider connections, adopt openai-agents-sdk's provider-advice protocol (`Model.get_retry_advice`) so transport specifics (headers, websocket phases) normalize into runner-consumable guidance (src/agents/models/interface.py:59-65).
4. Make transport retries opt-in but ship a documented baseline policy; default-off without a loud recipe equals default-fragile.
5. Decide replay safety per operation class and encode it fail-closed with an explicit escape hatch (openai-agents-sdk's veto algebra is the reference implementation).

**If you build a platform/service** (you take 3 a.m. pages):
1. Two-tier retries: declarative mechanical retries on jobs/queues, semantic retries as deliberate re-enqueues with persisted baggage — keeping the mechanical counter meaningful (langfuse).
2. Put a shared backoff primitive in a common package and consume it everywhere, even if scheduling loops stay bespoke (opa's `DefaultBackoff`); resist letta/crewai-style per-site formulas.
3. Scope circuit breakers to your natural blast-radius unit (namespace/destination for temporal; project for langfuse) and convert breaker-open into a protocol-level throttling signal downstream systems already understand.
4. Persist retry state for anything user-visible or billable; keep internal fast-path counters volatile but bounded.
5. Ship a DLQ with replay tooling for every queue that carries irreplaceable work; retention without replay is a museum, not a safety valve (langfuse's five-queue coverage gap illustrates the drift).
6. Choose fail-open vs. fail-closed per path and write the rationale down — langfuse's pairing (fail-open rate limiting, fail-closed S3 uploads) is the clearest articulation in the cohort.

**If you are choosing between the models for a new system:** Model A (engine-owned policy) whenever many heterogeneous operations share one execution seam; Model C (queue/timer durability) whenever work must survive crashes by contract; Model B (composition) only when dependency weight or provider diversity dominates — and then budget engineering time to write the canonical recipe yourself.

## Practical Tips

- **Classify errors once, centrally, and share the result.** The strongest implementations derive `isRetryable` from typed surfaces (langfuse `errors.ts:42-51`, temporal's `IsPersistenceTransientError` taxonomy at common/util.go:290-354, crewai's A2A whitelist of 408/409/429/5xx). Avoid string-sentinel matching (openhands compares exported error-message constants; langfuse's ClickHouse classifier sniffs substrings like `"socket hang up"` and its own report calls it brittle).
- **Always add jitter, and say why in a comment.** OpenHands justifies its ±30% WS jitter as keeping parallel sockets out of lockstep (src/hooks/use-websocket.ts:125-132); temporal embeds ±~20% automatically (retrypolicy.go:178-185). Jitter-free or delay-free loops appear exactly where resilience was bolted on (crewai's instant agent/guardrail/tool re-entry; agent-framework's linear `0.25s × attempt` Magentic sleeps, which would synchronize concurrent orchestrators into a retry storm).
- **Honor `Retry-After` end-to-end**, including HTTP-date form and ms-precision variants (pydantic-ai retries.py:514-588; openai-agents-sdk _retry_runtime.py:60-97). Give server hints precedence over local heuristics.
- **Separate quota exhaustion from throttling.** CrewAI's Brave tool treats 429 RATE_LIMITED as retryable but QUOTA_LIMITED as terminal because "retrying will never succeed until the billing period resets" (brave_search_tool/base.py:51-64) — a distinction its own LLM layer lacks.
- **Reset counters on success, count consecutive failures** (opa's `retry = 0` on success in every loop, download.go:252-256). Backoff should measure consecutive failures, not total age — except when protecting against long outages, where langfuse's age-budgeted ladder ("roughly 5m, 43m, 82m, 120m" spread over a wall-clock window with elapsed-time compensation) prevents unbounded backlog growth (retry-handler.ts:14-66, 132-150).
- **Make degradation recoverable or refuse it.** Openhands degrades with escape hatches (optimistic message → error state with retry link) and deliberately refuses to degrade where correctness matters (conversation-start credential fetches throw; transcript export fails loudly rather than exporting a partial tail, load-complete-events.ts:98-123).
- **Persist the minimum that makes restart safe, not the whole retry ledger**: ETags and last-known-good bundles (opa), ERROR-outcome pending writes (langgraph), `RetryBaggage` inside job payloads (langfuse). Full durable counters are warranted only when retries are part of the product contract (temporal).
- **Watchdog hangs into your existing error/retry UX** instead of inventing new failure paths — openhands' handshake watchdog closes stuck CONNECTING sockets so the normal reconnect loop proceeds (websocket-handshake.ts:1-26), and letta measures event-loop heartbeat lag from a separate thread so a frozen loop cannot hide itself (event_loop_watchdog.py:44-108).
- **When testing, mock the clock and assert exact schedules.** The pattern recurs in every high scorer (langgraph test_retry.py:249-306; openai-agents-sdk's monkeypatched jitter; temporal's exact-attempt DataLoss assertions). Also script provider advice in test doubles (openai-agents-sdk `ModelStep.raise_error(..., retry_advice=...)`).
- **Fold the cost of rejected attempts into observability**: pydantic-ai bills rejected fallback responses onto the winning usage (fallback.py:296-307); openai-agents-sdk records failed attempts as zero-token request entries (model_retry.py:318-350). Otherwise degradation silently distorts cost metrics.

## Anti-Patterns / Caution Signs

- **Copy-paste backoff idioms.** CrewAI's identical `wait_time = 2**attempt` in five files with no jitter; letta's ≥8 divergent implementations including sync `time.sleep` that would block event loops if called async. Caution sign: grep finds more than three backoff formulas in one repo.
- **Retrying definitive failures.** OpenHands' generic `withRetry` burns all 3 attempts (+~1.5s) on 401/400s that its own probe path correctly excludes — three different discrimination policies coexisting in one frontend. Caution sign: a retry helper that takes no error classifier argument.
- **Immediate re-entry on the hot path.** CrewAI's agent/guardrail/tool loops recurse instantly with zero delay — ineffective against rate limits and additive load during incidents.
- **Sample-only or test-only canonical behavior.** Agent-framework's sanctioned retry pattern lives in `samples/02-agents/auto_retry.py` and explicitly punts on streaming ("Streaming retry is more complex", auto_retry.py:82-84); crewai's streaming→non-streaming fallback appears only as a test that mocks `llm.call` itself — intent, not mechanism. Caution sign: the doc-recommended pattern isn't importable from the package.
- **Hardcoded knobs scattered across modules.** Letta's `max_retries=20` (429-only!) beside newer 3-attempt paths; langfuse's webhook `numOfAttempts: 4` and observation-retry constants beside env-configurable neighbors. Fleet-wide tuning becomes archaeology.
- **Silent capability substitution.** Letta's router stub and noop Redis client keep builds green while removing fallback, breaking, idempotency, and lock guarantees discovered only at runtime. Prefer opt-in-with-loud-docs (openai-agents-sdk style) over silent stubs.
- **Dead contract leftovers.** OpenHands' `STATUS$LLM_RETRY` has zero producers/consumers; if a backend emitted it, nothing would render. Unused resilience contracts rot into traps.
- **Breakers without half-open probing.** Openhands' disabled backends re-arm only via modal visits or config edits; a recovered backend stays marked dead indefinitely. Any trip mechanism needs an automatic or one-shot probing story (`probeDisabledOnce` is a patch, not a cure).
- **Protection default-off.** Letta's readiness gates and langfuse's slowdown breaker both ship disabled; the machinery existing is not the same as the safeguard operating. Caution sign: a resilience feature whose enablement requires remembering a flag.
- **Hidden multiplicative retries.** Agent-framework's unexposed SDK defaults wrapped by user middleware produce attempt-count multiplication nobody configured. If you can't answer "how many total attempts happen worst-case?", that number is probably larger than anyone chose.

## Notable Absences

- **Automatic fallback model/provider chains: absent in 8 of 9 LLM-touching sources.** Only pydantic-ai implements one; letta's is inert in OSS. Even multi-provider registries (openai-agents-sdk `MultiProvider`) route at resolution time only. This is the single largest gap against the dimension's guiding question.
- **General-purpose circuit breakers: absent in 7 of 10 sources**, and present only as bespoke point solutions where they exist (gobreaker pool, Redis TTL flags, localStorage disable cap). No source uses or ships a reusable breaker abstraction around arbitrary outbound calls.
- **Retry-specific spans/traces:** weak almost everywhere. Langfuse and letta emit metrics/events; langgraph and openai-agents-sdk log only; several reports explicitly could not verify whether OTel captures attempt numbers.
- **Streaming retry coverage:** agent-framework documents exclusion outright; pydantic-ai's response-content-based fallback doesn't apply to streams (docs/models/overview.md:410-412); openai-agents-sdk handles streaming best via pre/post-event retry windows (only `response.created`/`in_progress` are retry-safe, model_retry.py:384-386) — worth studying as the reference design.
- **Idempotency keys on retried mutations:** openhands found none despite wrapping writes in retry helpers.
- **Rate-limit auto-handling at engine layer:** langgraph types `RateLimitError(429)` for consumers but performs no automatic 429 retry — typed awareness without action.
- **Cross-provider health memory:** no source remembers across runs that a provider was failing (pydantic-ai re-probes the primary every run; its report proposes a stateful wrapper as future work).
- **DLQs in agent frameworks:** quarantine-and-replay is exclusive to platform sources (temporal, langfuse); frameworks raise to callers.

## Per-Source Notes

- **agent-framework (5):** Resilience distributed across four mechanisms — user middleware + tenacity sample, bounded orchestration parse-retries, a genuinely mature idempotency-aware MCP transport (proactive pings, one-shot reconnect, best-effort task cancel compensation), and .NET-only routable-degraded Foundry hosting. Fallbacks and breakers absent; streaming excluded from documented retry; fragmented knob vocabulary. Strongest single component: MCP transport (`_mcp.py:2108-2160`, tested at `tests/core/test_mcp.py:1347`).
- **crewai (6):** Ten-plus independent retry sites with good edge-case tests but inconsistent backoff (present in MCP/A2A/LanceDB, absent in agent/guardrail/tool cores). Best error classification thinking in the cohort (auth/not-found fail-fast in MCP; 429-vs-quota in Brave; A2A retryable whitelist). Degradation breadth notable: context summarization, param dropping, tracing-never-blocks, memory-sync fallback. Durability via checkpoints/flow persistence, not retry journals.
- **langfuse (8):** Reference architecture for queue-backed platforms: declarative BullMQ retries under age-budgeted jittered re-enqueues with zod-validated `RetryBaggage`; two TTL-keyed Redis breakers; secondary-queue isolation; DLQ cron; HTTP 207 partial success; metrics on every branch. Acknowledged soft spot: in-memory ClickHouse buffer drops data at exhaustion (TODO at index.ts:544). No model fallback.
- **langgraph (7):** Cleanest single retry API in study (`RetryPolicy` NamedTuple, three scopes, precedence, exception-predicate matchers, multiple policies per node). Unique restart semantics: failed tasks persist `(ERROR, exception)` writes and re-execute fresh on resume while handler routing survives via `ERROR_SOURCE_NODE`. Engineered exception taxonomy (`NodeTimeoutError` deliberately not an `OSError` so defaults treat timeouts as retryable). Gaps: no 429/Retry-After handling, no breaker, fresh budgets per crash, sync/async backoff math duplicated.
- **letta (6):** Pervasive but fragmented retries (decorator with 20× 429-only legacy path alongside modern classified clients); excellent semantic retry design (context-compaction loop, billing-aware fallback switching fully wired in the step loop); well-designed pod readiness/degradation subsystem — all gates default-off. The flagship `LLMRoutingClient` (fallback + circuit breaker) exists only as a noop stub in this tree; feature effectively enterprise-gated. Dedicated tests cover embeddings retry only.
- **opa (7):** Answers the guiding question most completely relative to its domain: bundle-service outages never touch query evaluation (last-known-good activation, pinned by test), decision logs shed load via bounded drop-oldest buffers with counters, encoding degrades via ND-cache shedding before dropping. One shared gRPC-style backoff helper (factor 1.6, ±20% jitter) with reset-on-success counters; protocol fallback (long-poll→polling). No breakers, no endpoint failover, hard-coded base delays, memory-only log buffers.
- **openai-agents-sdk (8):** Most sophisticated retry *safety* model in study: policy-as-code callables with veto-aware combinators, three fail-closed replay vetoes with explicit approval escape, provider-advice protocol, hidden provider-retry coordination via ContextVars, session rewind with fingerprint verification, per-attempt usage accounting, delay precedence chain. Trade-offs: zero default retries, static multi-provider routing, observability limited to debug logs, cognitively heavy coordination matrices.
- **openhands (6):** Frontend-scoped resilience: jittered WS reconnect with handshake watchdog and replaced-socket suppression; persisted coarse backend breaker (5 strikes, localStorage, tamper-validated); many individually sound, well-commented degradation decisions (degrade-to-defaults settings vs. throw-on-conversation-credentials asymmetry). Held back by indiscriminate `withRetry` (duplicated verbatim, retries 401s), infinite WS retry default, string-sentinel classification, no half-open probing, and a dead `STATUS$LLM_RETRY` literal. LLM-tier resilience explicitly delegated to sibling repos.
- **pydantic-ai (8):** Only source with production fallback providers: `FallbackModel` chain with customizable triggers (exception/response handlers auto-detected via type hints), empty-policy guard, `FallbackExceptionGroup` aggregation, cost accounting of rejected responses, span correction to actual serving model, continuation pinning with rewind for suspended streams. Transport retries opt-in via tenacity with `Retry-After`-aware waits. Semantic retries as conversation (`RetryPromptPart`) with per-tool/output budgets, counter hygiene, negative-budget hardening. Missing: breakers, cross-run health, streaming semantic fallback, default transport resilience.
- **temporal (9):** Cohort benchmark. Reusable policy hierarchy (exponential/constant/error-dependent/disabled/conditional/throttle) consumed by decorator clients at every boundary; durable user-facing retries as timer tasks with persisted attempts and typed `RetryState` outcomes; application-controlled `NextRetryDelay` override; gobreaker destination pool normalizing outages to `ResourceExhausted(CIRCUIT_BREAKER_OPEN)`; DLQ with dynamic-config governance; error masking and read-only-DB-as-transient at the edges. Residual nits: duplicated jitter impls, breaker state lost on settings hot-swap, volatile in-memory task counters.
