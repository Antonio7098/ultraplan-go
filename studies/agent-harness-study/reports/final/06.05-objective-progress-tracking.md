## Executive Summary

Eight sources, one shared verdict: **every system in this study has solved mechanical progress and none has solved semantic progress.** Seven of eight score 7/10 and temporal scores 9/10 — a remarkably tight band that reflects a converged understanding of what is tractable. All eight can tell you precisely how much work happened: agent-framework counts rounds/stalls/resets (`_magentic.py:344-346`) and injects a "Progress so far:" log back into context (`_loop.py:798-842`); langgraph stamps every checkpoint with a superstep number (`checkpoint/base/__init__.py:41-55`); letta writes PENDING step rows before each LLM call and finalizes them with nanosecond timing metrics (`step_manager.py:419-476`, `step_metrics.py:13-26`); openai-agents-sdk keeps runner-owned turn counters the model cannot touch (`run_loop.py:1536-1537`); pydantic-ai enforces budget limits at five distinct boundaries (`usage.py:492-573`); openhands streams `Round N/M` with a judge score to the UI (`goal-status-content.tsx:130-166`); temporal makes every step an immutable history event that survives rebuild-from-history (`mutable_state_rebuilder.go:72-135`). What no system does by default is independently verify that reaching "done" means the goal was achieved.

The dimension's framing question — *does the agent know the difference between activity and progress?* — gets an honest "no" from most sources and a partial "yes" from three. CrewAI is the only framework that pushes anti-narration into its prompts ("exploratory ls/pwd/cat without producing the required artifact" must be marked incomplete, `en.json:88`). OpenHands is the only source where a separate judge's verdict — not the working agent's claim — gates termination, with a mandatory `missing` field recording what remains (`conversation-state-event.ts:68-75`). Agent-framework dedicates two of its five ledger slots to exactly this distinction (`is_in_loop`, `is_progress_being_made`, `_magentic.py:307-334`). Everywhere else, activity and achievement are conflated at the type level: letta maps `max_steps`, `tool_rule`, and `end_turn` all to `RunStatus.completed` (`letta_stop_reason.py:24-32`), so truncation is indistinguishable from success to polling clients.

The spread of approaches reflects product shape more than quality. Libraries (pydantic-ai, openai-agents-sdk, langgraph) push goal semantics to developers and keep the runtime honest about structure. Products and platforms (letta, openhands, temporal) own more of the loop and therefore own more of the completion decision. Orchestration frameworks (agent-framework, crewai) sit between, adding LLM-judged progress layers on top of structural bounds. Temporal earns its 9 not by knowing the goal better but by making the weakest semantic claims while making every structural claim unfakeable, auditable, and recoverable.

## Core Thesis

**The field measures distance-to-done by proxy, and the choice of proxy is the architecture.** Since no harness can inspect user intent, each source substitutes one of three proxies for "are we closer?": a *second opinion* (an LLM judge re-reads the trajectory each round — agent-framework's Magentic ledger, crewai's PlannerObserver, openhands' GoalVerdict), a *structural fact* (typed output validated, no pending tool calls, exit-tool invoked — pydantic-ai's `End[FinalResult]`, openai-agents-sdk's response classification, letta's continuation rules), or *user code* (conditional edges — langgraph, where goal semantics are literally unreachable by the framework).

These proxies have opposite failure modes. Second opinions are semantically rich but spoofable and expensive: a hallucinated `is_request_satisfied=true` ends a Magentic workflow with a synthesized answer (`_magentic.py:1110-1114`), and crewai's observer green-lights steps when its own LLM call fails (`planner_observer.py:208-213`). Structural facts are tamper-resistant but blind: a confidently wrong answer that type-checks is a successful pydantic-ai run, and a plain-text OpenAI Agents SDK run terminates with zero verification unless the developer attaches guardrails (`guardrail.py:133-152`). Temporal is the proof that the highest score goes to the system that picks structural facts and then hardens them beyond forgery — token-bound heartbeats (`recordactivitytaskheartbeat/api.go:25-75`), command-sequence validation (`command_attr_validator.go:637`), first-wins completion (`workflow_task_completed_handler.go:821-830`), irreversible terminal states (`mutable_state_state_status.go:74-92`) — while explicitly refusing to judge semantics (`command_attr_validator.go:219-228` accepts any non-nil result payload).

The practical consequence: **trust boundaries are configuration, not code, in every source.** Whether a model can fake progress depends entirely on which knobs the integrator set — whether todos require evidence (they don't, `_todo.py:540-561`), whether output guardrails exist (opt-in), whether heartbeat timeouts were configured (opt-in, `timer_sequence.go:420-423`), whether a terminal tool rule was registered (then invoking it ends the run as success). The mature move, visible in the strongest sources, is not to eliminate self-reporting but to make every non-self-reported quantity harness-owned and every terminus honestly typed.

## Approach Models

### Model A — Judged Ledger (second-opinion loop)

**Sources:** agent-framework (Magentic), crewai (PlannerObserver), openhands (goal judge).

Each round, a dedicated judgment produces a structured verdict: Magentic's manager fills a five-slot `MagenticProgressLedger` from forced JSON with retries (`_magentic.py:194-243, 307-334`); crewai's observer emits `StepObservation(step_completed_successfully, needs_full_replan, goal_already_achieved)` (`planner_observer.py:113-213`); openhands' server judge returns `GoalVerdict{score, complete, missing}` (`conversation-state-event.ts:66-75`). Verdicts drive routing — replan/refine/continue/goal-achieved (crewai `agent_executor.py:848-930`), stall-triggered reset+replan (agent-framework `_magentic.py:1116-1125`), or terminate-with-verdict (openhands).

Strength: these are the only approaches that can say "we ran but got nowhere" and act on it. Weakness: the judge is another LLM — correlated with the worker, parseable-output-dependent, and priced per round. Agent-framework mitigates parse drift with fail-safe direction (ambiguous judge text means MORE work, `_loop.py:192-201`) and reset budgets; crewai leaves its fallback directions inconsistent (observer outage → optimistic continue, unparseable JSON → pessimistic failure).

### Model B — Harness-Arbitrated Structure (rules over the loop)

**Sources:** letta, openai-agents-sdk, pydantic-ai, temporal.

Continuation/completion is decided by harness code from structural facts: letta's `_decide_continuation` reads tool-call presence, terminal tool rules, and required-before-exit tools (`letta_agent_v3.py:1967-2036`), and v3 strips `request_heartbeat` so the model cannot pace its own loop (`letta_agent_v3.py:1776, 2071`); openai-agents-sdk classifies each response into `NextStepFinalOutput/Handoff/Interruption/RunAgain` (`run_steps.py:161-199`); pydantic-ai ends runs only at `End[FinalResult]` after schema+validator survival (`run.py:143-161`, `_output.py:121-173`); temporal validates commands structurally and enforces a total state-transition matrix (`mutable_state_state_status.go:16-125`).

Strength: counters, statuses, and terminations cannot be written by the model — they are mechanically derived. This is why four of the eight sources can answer "can the model fake progress?" with "mechanically, no." Weakness: none of these systems knows what the work meant; success reduces to shape.

### Model C — Application-Owned Semantics (framework as accountant)

**Sources:** langgraph (pure case); partially crewai (whose `expected_output` is prompt text, never machine-compared, `task.py:1092-1101`).

LangGraph tracks supersteps, checkpoints, and scheduler-derived events with unusual rigor, but "the goal exists implicitly as the graph's terminal structure" (`constants.py:28-29`, conditional edges at `graph/state.py:982-1030`). Its own report concludes: whether a superstep moved toward the goal "is unknowable to the framework."

Strength: maximal flexibility and zero premature commitment to a goal model; plan-and-execute, reflexion, etc., build cleanly in user code. Weakness: the flagship prebuilt agent's "done" is whatever the model decides by omitting tool calls (`chat_agent_executor.py:834-841`), and graphs can spin useless cycles until the high default guard trips.

## Pattern Catalog

### Pattern 1 — Bounded Autonomy Everywhere

**Problem:** loops that neither the model nor the framework can recognize as pointless.
**Demonstrated by:** all eight. Numeric caps: rounds/stalls/resets (agent-framework `_magentic.py:1229-1265`), `max_iter=25` + replan caps (crewai `agent_executor.py:197, 1019-1027`), `recursion_limit=10007` (langgraph `_internal/_config.py:32`), `DEFAULT_MAX_STEPS=50` (letta `constants.py:75`), `DEFAULT_MAX_TURNS=10` (openai-agents-sdk `run_config.py:45`), `max_iterations` with explicit `capped` terminus (openhands), `request_limit=50` defaults (pydantic-ai `usage.py:417-472`), heartbeat timeouts (temporal `timer_sequence.go:405-444`).
**Why it works:** converts an undecidable question ("is this productive?") into a decidable one ("have we spent N?").
**Copy when:** always. It is the cheapest correct safeguard in the study.
**Overkill/risky when:** caps substitute for progress thinking. LangGraph's 10007 default permits large wasted spend before intervention, and letta reports hitting the cap as `completed`.
**Evidence:** every rating rationale cites its cap plus tests exercising it.

### Pattern 2 — Structured Verdict Payloads for Model Judgment

**Problem:** free-text self-assessment is unusable as control flow.
**Demonstrated by:** `MagenticProgressLedger` (reason+answer per slot, `_magentic.py:307-334`), `JudgeVerdict(answered, reasoning)` (`_loop.py:104-116`), `StepObservation` (crewai `planner_observer.py:113-213`), `GoalVerdict{score, complete, missing}` (openhands `conversation-state-event.ts:66-75`).
**Why it works:** forces the judgment through a schema, gives every decision a mandatory reason string (audit trail), and enables branching (`decide_next_action`'s four futures in crewai).
**Copy when:** you need model judgment to drive control flow — type the verdict, require the reason, bound the retries (agent-framework uses up-to-3 parse retries with backoff, `_magentic.py:722-735`).
**Risky when:** the verdict parser becomes the single point of failure; agent-framework handles this by treating unparseable ledgers as a stall signal feeding reset+replan rather than crashing (`_magentic.py:1086-1092`).

### Pattern 3 — Harness-Owned Counters, Model-Owned Claims (deliberate trust split)

**Problem:** models will narrate success regardless of reality.
**Demonstrated by:** openai-agents-sdk runner-accounted turns/usage/tool identity (`run_loop.py:2306`, `tool_use_tracker.py:53-90`); pydantic-ai counting only *successful* tool executions (`tool_manager.py:1025`); letta stripping `request_heartbeat` (`letta_agent_v3.py:1776`); temporal rejecting heartbeats without valid task tokens bound to live activities (`recordactivitytaskheartbeat/api.go:25-75`); agent-framework deriving background-task status from real `asyncio.Task` state including a `LOST` status for vanished tasks (`_background_agents.py:214-259`); langgraph emitting stream events only from actual task scheduling/writes (`_loop.py:1416-1466`).
**Why it works:** draws a line between what may be self-reported (task content) and what is measured (execution mechanics), so event-level faking is structurally impossible even where content-level faking remains possible.
**Copy when:** building any progress surface consumed by humans or billing.
**Limit:** the split protects mechanics, not meaning — temporal's busy-looping worker can still emit valid heartbeats while doing nothing useful.

### Pattern 4 — Honest Failure Termini at the Type Level

**Problem:** clients conflate exhaustion/interruption with achievement.
**Demonstrated by:** openhands distinguishing `complete` vs `capped` vs `interrupted`, rendered green-check vs muted-cross (`goal-status-content.tsx:146-159`); pydantic-ai raising `IncompleteToolCall` so truncation is distinguished from retry exhaustion (`_agent_graph.py:345-359`); temporal closing failed attempts as new runs under retry/cron policy instead of mutating closed runs (`workflow_task_completed_handler.go:887-916`); crewai's `CrewOutput.tool_failures` documenting that "a crew can finish successfully" over failed steps (`crew_output.py:35-47`).
**Anti-pattern counterpoint:** letta mapping `max_steps`→`completed` (`letta_stop_reason.py:26-32`) and agent-framework delivering round-limit terminations as normal-looking prose through the same channel as answers (`_magentic.py:1255-1261`).
**Copy when:** designing any status enum — make budget-exhaustion, interruption, and achievement distinct values from day one; retrofitting (as letta's own future-work list admits) is harder than shipping it.

### Pattern 5 — Blockers as First-Class Durable State

**Problem:** paused/blocked runs vanish across restarts or get misread as failures.
**Demonstrated by:** openai-agents-sdk serializing whole runs into versioned `RunState` with approve/reject/resume and refusing input into blocked states (`run_state.py:170-182, 985-1017`); langgraph persisting interrupts via reserved-channel writes so multi-interrupt resume works across processes (`_internal/_constants.py:9-12`, tests `test_pregel.py:7577`); letta's persisted `ApprovalRequestMessage` + `requires_approval` stop reason (`letta_agent_v3.py:1682-1709`); pydantic-ai encoding blockers as `RetryPromptPart`s in replayable history plus resumable `DeferredToolRequests` (`messages.py:1637-1697`); temporal's typed failure causes and pending-activity fields with next-attempt scheduling (`activity.go:116-134`).
**Why it works:** blockers recorded in the same write pipeline as normal state survive crashes deterministically.
**Copy when:** human-in-the-loop or long-running work exists anywhere in the product.
**Cost:** client bookkeeping (langgraph requires an interrupt-id map when multiple interrupts pend, `_loop.py:916-920`) and API complexity.

### Pattern 6 — Progress Fed Back Into Context

**Problem:** agents lose the thread of their own trajectory, repeating work or drifting.
**Demonstrated by:** agent-framework rendering "Progress so far:" logs and todo lists into subsequent inputs (`_loop.py:798-842`, `_todo.py:596-614`), including a fresh-context mode where the progress log is the *only* continuity carrier (`_loop.py:431-436`); crewai feeding guardrail errors verbatim into the retry context (`task.py:1394-1408`); letta rendering `<tool_rule>` explanations when forcing continuation (`letta_agent_v3.py:1994-1997`); langgraph injecting `RemainingSteps`/`IsLastStep` managed values so nodes degrade gracefully near the ceiling (`managed/is_last_step.py:18-21`).
**Why it works:** makes the progress ledger load-bearing for behavior, not just observability — which also raises the cost of corrupting it.
**Copy when:** loops exceed a handful of iterations or context resets occur mid-run.

### Pattern 7 — Checkpointed/Event-Sourced Progress Enabling Resume

**Problem:** crash or restart erases how far you got.
**Demonstrated by:** temporal rebuilding mutable state by replaying history (`mutable_state_rebuilder.go:72-135`); langgraph's monotonic uuid6 checkpoint IDs keyed on step with chained write ordering (`checkpoint/base/__init__.py:854`, `_loop.py:1199-1209`); agent-framework checkpointing task+progress ledgers (`_magentic.py:1273-1329`); crewai serializing `_completed_methods` into flow checkpoints (`flow/runtime/__init__.py:682-703`); openai-agents-sdk's schema-versioned `to_json/from_json` round-trip (`run_state.py:2174`); pydantic-ai's `RunCancelled` snapshot preserving messages+usage (`exceptions.py:268-330`).
**Why it works:** progress becomes reconstructable rather than merely observable — the difference between auditing a claim and trusting one.
**Caveat:** agent-framework swallows checkpoint-restore failures as warnings (`_magentic.py:1299-1328`), silently losing the progress baseline — restoration paths need the same rigor as write paths.

### Pattern 8 — Anti-Narration Prompting Against Fake Progress

**Problem:** models mark exploration as completion ("I listed the directory" ≠ artifact produced).
**Demonstrated by:** crewai's planning prompts instructing observers to fail steps whose result "is only exploratory (ls, pwd, cat) without producing the required artifact" (`translations/en.json:88`) and requiring outcome verification before Final Answer (`en.json:90, 96`); openhands' `GoalVerdict.missing` forcing the judge to name what remains (`conversation-state-event.ts:70-75`); agent-framework's dedicated `is_in_loop`/`is_progress_being_made` slots (`_magentic.py:307-334`).
**Why it works:** cheap — moves part of the activity-vs-progress distinction into the prompt contract where the judgment already happens.
**Copy when:** running judged-ledger loops. It is the only pattern found that targets narration-driven false completion directly.

## Key Differences

- **Who says "done":** OpenHands terminates on a separate judge's verdict — the working agent's claim is architecturally insufficient evidence (`conversation-state-event.ts:66-75`). Agent-framework and crewai also use judges but trust them inside the same orchestration family (Magentic's manager judges its own participants, `_magentic.py:699-739`). Letta, openai-agents-sdk, pydantic-ai, and temporal refuse model-declared completion entirely in favor of structural rules. LangGraph outsources the decision to user code. Source A (agent-framework) uses an in-family manager because multi-agent coordination needs per-round routing decisions; Source B (letta) uses structural rules because a chat-product server needs predictable, cheap, testable termination.
- **What faking means:** In langgraph, an agent can mislabel state (`plan: [...all complete]`) but cannot fabricate stream-visible execution (`output_writes` requires real task writes, `_loop.py:1416-1466`). In openhands, the inverse holds: the task-tracker list is pure narration the UI faithfully displays (`use-task-list.ts:39-44`), while the goal loop's execution accounting is real. In temporal both halves are real but semantics remain self-reported. Comparing these shows "fake progress" decomposes into three independent axes — event fabrication, state mislabeling, and semantic overclaim — and each source closes different subsets.
- **Terminus honesty:** OpenHands and pydantic-ai distinguish exhaustion from completion at the type level (`capped`; `IncompleteToolCall`); letta and agent-framework conflate them (status mapping; prose termination messages). This is not maturity theater — it directly determines whether monitoring dashboards, polling clients, and postmortems can tell truncated runs from achieved ones.
- **Where progress lives:** durable stores (letta DB rows, temporal history, langgraph checkpoints) vs serialized run state (openai-agents-sdk) vs ephemeral projections (openhands feeds its goal store only from WebSocket handlers, so reload loses the live banner, `conversation-websocket-context.tsx:652-654`). Product shape drives this: a frontend legitimately projects; a workflow engine must durably own.
- **Observability philosophy:** openai-agents-sdk exposes four redundant channels (streams, hooks, spans, result fields) so consumers pick their level; letta fans out one terminal event to four observers (SSE, callback, webhook, rollups, `run_manager.py:332-439`); langgraph offers seven stream modes plus a beta consolidation; agent-framework curates what crosses the `as_agent()` boundary, hiding internal progress events by default (`_events.py:133-143`). More surface is not better — filtered-by-default surfaces hide exactly the evidence this dimension cares about unless callers drop down a level.

## Tradeoffs

**LLM-judged progress (Model A) vs structural arbitration (Model B).**
Benefit (A): detects meaningless activity, enables mid-run replanning, produces human-readable reasons. Cost (A): extra calls per round (Magentic's ledger runs up to 3 parse retries, `_magentic.py:588-590`), judge variance, correlated bias (crewai's evaluator may share the workers' model lineage), spoofable verdicts. Best-fit: multi-agent orchestration and long autonomous runs where replanning beats restarting. Failure mode: judge outage or drift silently reroutes the run (crewai's optimistic fallback). Alternative (B): deterministic, cheap, testable — but blind to meaning; best-fit for libraries and products where predictability is the product.

**Structural simplicity vs goal fidelity.**
Benefit: openai-agents-sdk's "completion is a property of response shape" makes termination predictable and provider-independent. Cost: no vocabulary for "80% done," sub-goals, or ordered milestones — integrators build that above `new_items`. Pydantic-ai states the same split deliberately: run-time tracking and evaluation are separate packages, and the loop never consults evaluators.

**Durability vs consistency.**
Temporal decouples authoritative state from query/UI projection via async visibility tasks, gaining isolation of expensive indexing at the cost of eventual consistency — a just-closed workflow can briefly appear open (`visibility_queue_task_executor.go:236-303`). LangGraph makes the opposite bet: checkpoints chain synchronously so history reads are ordered. Choose per read-path tolerance.

**Availability vs integrity.**
Letta logs-but-applies illegal run transitions (`run_manager.py:341-356`) and fails-closed on missing stream sentinels (`streaming_service.py:777-800`) — availability for lifecycle integrity, integrity for liveness. Temporal rejects invalid commands outright and suppresses duplicates (`workflow_task_completed_handler.go:790-793, 821-830`). The letta trade suits interactive chat; the temporal trade suits financial-grade workflows.

**Conflation for API simplicity.**
Mapping every loop exit to `completed` (letta) keeps client APIs trivial and shifts interpretation duty to `stop_reason` readers. The cost is invisible until a naive polling client treats a 50-step truncation as achievement — the exact failure letta's own report flags first.

**Self-reported checklists vs verified ones.**
OpenHands gets flexible decomposition and rich UI for free by letting the model author its `task_tracker` list, forfeiting ground truth; its own future-work proposes cross-checking `done` flips against observations. Agent-framework's todos likewise accept any non-empty reason (`_todo.py:540-561`) — though faking completion there at least ends the run, converting fraud into a bounded audit trail.

## Decision Guide

- **Building a library or SDK?** Use Model B: structural completion against a typed output contract, runner-owned counters, opt-in semantic gates. Copy openai-agents-sdk's classification + guardrail split ("the SDK verifies form; developers verify meaning") and pydantic-ai's boundary-by-boundary limit enforcement. Do not bake in a judge; expose the hook.
- **Orchestrating multiple agents on a fuzzy objective?** Use Model A with the full safety kit: typed verdict schemas, stall counters with hysteresis (decay on recovery, `_magentic.py:1117-1120`), reset budgets, and fail-safe parsing direction biased toward continuing work.
- **Shipping a product where users watch runs?** Give the goal first-class wire representation with per-round verdicts and honest terminal states (openhands model): `missing` on every verdict, `capped` distinct from `complete`, visual grammar that refuses to celebrate incomplete outcomes. Then fix what openhands hasn't: persist active-goal state so reload doesn't hide a running loop.
- **Workflows where records must be unfakeable and auditable?** Temporal model: append-only event sourcing, total state-transition function, token-bound liveness, first-wins completion. Accept that you must add your own acceptance checks on result payloads — the server verifies structure, you verify correctness.
- **Maximal flexibility, goals defined per-application?** LangGraph model: mechanical superstep progress, durable checkpoints, goal logic in conditional edges. Budget-awareness (`RemainingSteps`) belongs in node-visible managed values.
- **Chat-product server with human-in-the-loop?** Letta model minus the conflation: enumerated stop reasons, harness-owned continuation, approval-as-pause — but split `completed` from `truncated`/`blocked` at the enum level before clients accrete onto the conflated one.
- **Rule of thumb:** choose your proxy for "progress" first (judge / structure / user code), then harden everything that isn't self-reported, then make the self-reported parts cheap to audit afterward. Sources that did these in this order score higher than sources with richer features in a different order.

## Practical Tips

- **Make counters harness property.** Turn numbers, token totals, executed-tool identities, and step statuses should be computed from actual executions, never accepted from model output (openai-agents-sdk `tool_use_tracker.py:53-90`; pydantic-ai counting successes only, `tool_manager.py:1025`).
- **Type your verdicts and require reasons.** Every judged decision should be `{answer, reason}` minimum (Magentic slots, JudgeVerdict, StepObservation, GoalVerdict all do this). Reasons double as blocker records.
- **Choose your fail-safe direction explicitly and document it.** Ambiguous judge output → keep looping (agent-framework `_loop.py:192-201`); unparseable ledger → treat as stall and replan (`_magentic.py:1086-1092`); dead stream → force-fail the run (`streaming_service.py:777-800`). CrewAI shows what happens when two fallbacks disagree in direction (observer outage → optimistic; parse failure → pessimistic).
- **Distinguish truncation from exhaustion from success in the status vocabulary.** Steal `capped` (openhands), `IncompleteToolCall` (pydantic-ai), and temporal's close-status metrics keyed by terminus kind.
- **Feed progress back into the model's context.** Progress logs, open-todo nudges, tool-rule explanations, and remaining-step budgets all measurably anchor behavior (Pattern 6) — and fresh-context modes prove their worth by making the log the sole continuity carrier.
- **Record what-missing alongside done/not-done.** `GoalVerdict.missing` is the study's best small idea: a blocker record generated by the same act that judges completion.
- **Pair self-reported mechanisms with a bound.** Letta explicitly advises pairing background-task predicates with `max_iterations` because persisted status may never refresh (`_loop.py:871-874`); generalize the advice — any predicate reading possibly-stale state needs a numeric partner.
- **Delay until needed:** milestone/percent primitives, in-loop evaluators, and cost-based loop bounds all appear in future-work sections across sources. None is needed on day one; all become expensive retrofits once external clients depend on the naive status surface.

## Anti-Patterns / Caution Signs

- **Success-shaped outputs over failed steps.** CrewAI documents it in its own result model: crews "finish successfully" while narrating failed steps (`crew_output.py:36-42`). If your success criterion is "non-empty output appeared," you have shipped this.
- **Truncation reported as completion.** Letta's `max_steps`→`completed` mapping means naive pollers celebrate cut-off runs (`letta_stop_reason.py:26-32`); agent-framework's prose termination ("Workflow terminated due to reaching maximum reset count.") rides the same channel as answers (`_magentic.py:1255-1261`).
- **Optimistic fallbacks in observers.** An infrastructure error inside the judge becoming `step_completed_successfully=True` (crewai `planner_observer.py:208-213`) means flakiness masquerades as progress — the worst possible failure direction for a measurement component.
- **Advisory-only invariant enforcement.** Logging illegal state transitions instead of rejecting them (letta `run_manager.py:341-356`) permits history rewriting after terminal states; once writers multiply, advisory guards rot.
- **Text-prefix matching as protocol.** OpenHands filters injected goal re-prompts by matching their literal text, labeled "Brittle by design" in-source (`should-render-event.ts:15-26`) — a genuine user message starting with the same words vanishes from transcripts.
- **Status-enum sprawl.** Four coexisting status enums with acknowledged consolidation debt (letta `enums.py:164-173`), STUCK flattened into ERROR downstream (openhands `use-agent-state.ts:30-31`): when consumers merge your distinctions, you no longer have distinctions.
- **Warning-degradation on enforcement.** Unpriced cost models silently downgrading `cost_limit` enforcement to a warning (pydantic-ai `usage.py:528-535`) is visible-but-missable — enforcement that can quietly become advice will.
- **Caution signs a design area is going brittle:** progress surfaces readable only by dropping below public APIs (agent-framework's filtered `as_agent()` events); reload/resume paths that lose live state (openhands banner); evaluation machinery that can itself crash the run (crewai evaluator exceptions); duplicated route registrations on metric endpoints (letta `runs.py:203-231`) — maintenance drift in the smallest surfaces predicts drift in the invariants.

## Notable Absences

Across all eight sources, the following are absent everywhere, not just somewhere:

- **Test-execution-based progress at runtime.** No source measures progress by running the thing being built inside the loop. OpenHands searched and confirmed the absence in its event stream; agent-framework's eval framework is CI-oriented and experimental (`@experimental` at `_evaluation.py:68-70`); crewai's `Crew.test()` and pydantic-ai's `pydantic_evals` are opt-in and out-of-loop; openhands' E2E framework validates the pipeline, not user tasks. The strongest form of independent checking — executable criteria feeding verdicts — appears only as openhands' future-work suggestion.
- **Independent final-success verification in the default path.** Every default path terminates on model judgment, structural rules, or app code. Where verification exists it is optional (guardrails, evaluators, judges), out-of-band (separate packages), or semantic-but-ungrounded (LLM judges reading claims).
- **Milestone/percent-complete primitives.** Searched and absent in multiple sources (agent-framework, openai-agents-sdk, pydantic-ai, letta); temporal lists surfacing one as future work. Progress granularity is uniformly binary-at-the-top (running/done) with activity counters underneath.
- **Reconciliation of model-authored checklists against observed work.** No source cross-checks a todo/task item marked done against intervening observations or artifacts (explicit gap in openhands; implicit in agent-framework's reason-string-only validation).
- **Grounded progress judgments.** Magentic's assessment is conversational only — grounded in chat history, not side-effect verification (`_magentic.py:719-723`); no source's in-loop judgment incorporates tool outcomes directly (e.g., "the file now exists").

## Per-Source Notes

- **agent-framework (7):** Richest mechanism inventory — five-layer stack (Magentic ledgers, judge loop, todos, background registry, offline evals) with tests at every layer. Loses points for the shared ceiling: model-judged completion, spoofable todos, prose-conflated terminations, offline-only independent checks.
- **crewai (7):** Most honest self-assessment in the study — documents its own success-theater risk in the result model and targets narration directly in prompts. Goal-proximity routing (`goal_achieved`/`replan_now`/`refine_and_continue`/`continue_plan`) is the clearest "are we closer?" branch point found anywhere.
- **langgraph (7):** Purest separation of mechanics from semantics. Answer to the framing question is explicit: mechanically distinguishes activity, has no native notion of meaningful progress. Interrupt persistence and multi-interrupt resume are the best-tested blocker machinery in the study.
- **letta (7):** Strongest harness-owns-outcome stance — v3 removes model-paced continuation entirely — undermined by the completed-conflation and advisory lifecycle guards. Fail-closed `[DONE]` watchdog and write-ahead step rows are production-grade touches.
- **openai-agents-sdk (7):** Cleanest statement of the form/meaning split; best blocker ergonomics (versioned resumable `RunState`, enforced blocked-state constraints, handler-parity testing across streamed/non-streamed paths).
- **openhands (7):** Only first-class goal object with per-round external audit and honest visual grammar; scoped to the frontend, so the judge's grounding is unverifiable here. Reload-during-loop and fakeable task tracker are the concrete gaps.
- **pydantic-ai (7):** Deepest resource-enforcement discipline (five boundary checks, projected tool-batch usage, chunk-level mid-stream checks); best accounting hygiene (successful-only tool counts, unknown-vs-zero cost). Semantic success deliberately exiled to `pydantic_evals`.
- **temporal (9):** The outlier score. Wins on what others don't attempt: structural verification of every claim, irreversibility of terminals, replay-authoritative state, duplicate suppression, liveness as configured timeouts. Its one lost point is the same ceiling everyone shares — semantics trusted to authors — accepted openly as design.

## Open Questions

- **What grounds the openhands judge?** The contract shows `score/complete/missing` but the judge's inputs live in the out-of-scope agent-server; whether "provably done" involves any non-LLM evidence is unknowable from this source.
- **Do platform/cloud layers add goal tracking above these primitives?** Letta annotates several fields "(cloud only)" with no local implementation; LangGraph Platform/UI dashboards and DevUI's rendering of Magentic progress payloads could not be confirmed from the studied repositories.
- **Can online evaluations gate (fail) runs, or only observe?** Pydantic-ai's `OnlineEvaluation` appears observational; if gating is possible it would close the structural-vs-semantic gap in-run.
- **Who reconciles orphaned `running` background runs?** Letta's foreground watchdog force-fails dead streams, but background-run recovery after processor death was not found in-source; a root-level `test_watchdog_hang.py` hints at awareness.
- **Does step-level feedback data feed execution anywhere?** Letta collects positive/negative feedback on steps but no consumer was found — currently a data-collection affordance, not a signal.
- **Would evaluator-backed `should_continue` predicates change the 7-band?** Agent-framework's own future-work proposes wiring its eval layer into in-loop gating; if any source ships that, it would be the first with in-loop independent verification and likely the first 8+ on this dimension outside temporal.
