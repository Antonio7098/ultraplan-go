# Dimension 01.13: Transition Linearization and Publication Semantics

## Purpose

Study the exact point at which state, outcome, timing, events, waiter release, and observer notification become authoritative and observable.

## Steps

1. Locate every lifecycle transition commit path.
2. Identify the serialization or linearization point.
3. Trace publication to readers, waiters, history, and observers.
4. Look for partially visible state and reordered notification.
5. Inspect memory-ordering, locking, and adverse-interleaving tests.

## Evidence to Capture

- Transition commit functions
- Locks, atomics, channels, and single-writer loops
- Snapshot and outcome publication
- Event append and notification ordering
- Race, stress, and invariant tests

## Questions

1. What single operation makes a transition authoritative?
2. Can any consumer observe only part of a transition?
3. Are outcome, event, timestamp, and waiter release consistent?
4. Does notification occur before or after canonical recording?
5. Can scheduler order change published meaning?
