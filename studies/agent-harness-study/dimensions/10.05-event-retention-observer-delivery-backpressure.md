# Dimension 10.05: Event Retention, Observer Delivery, and Backpressure

## Purpose

Study how an in-process runtime retains, replays, and delivers lifecycle events to multiple observers without allowing consumers to block execution.

## Steps

1. Determine whether events are retained, streamed, or both.
2. Identify the canonical event history and its owner.
3. Inspect how observers attach, resume, advance cursors, and detach.
4. Check how late observers receive historical events and transition to live delivery.
5. Determine how slow or abandoned observers affect producers and other observers.
6. Identify the backpressure, buffering, overflow, and drop policies.
7. Inspect how terminal closure and stream completion are represented.
8. Compare guarantees for canonical recording with guarantees for observer delivery.
9. Check whether event payloads are immutable or defensively copied.
10. Determine when retained history, observer state, and delivery buffers are released.

## Evidence to Capture

- Retained event logs, ring buffers, queues, or channels
- Subscription and cursor APIs
- Snapshot-plus-live handoff logic
- Observer registration and detachment code
- Buffer limits and overflow policies
- Slow-consumer and abandoned-consumer handling
- Terminal markers and stream closure logic
- Event copying, freezing, or immutable payload types
- Retention cleanup and lifecycle tests

## Questions

1. Is canonical history retained, streamed, or both?
2. Who owns observer cursors and delivery state?
3. Can late observers replay without gaps or duplicates at the live boundary?
4. Can one observer block execution or delay other observers?
5. What happens when buffers fill or observers stop consuming?
6. Is delivery push-based, pull-based, notification-based, or cursor-based?
7. How is terminal closure represented and observed?
8. Which guarantees apply to recording, replay, and live delivery respectively?
9. Can observers mutate payloads seen by the runtime or other observers?
10. When is retained history released?

## Rating

| Score | Meaning |
|-------|---------|
| 1-3 | Absent, implicit, ad-hoc, or unsafe |
| 4-6 | Present but inconsistent, weakly documented, or fragile |
| 7-8 | Clear model with tests, explicit interfaces, and operational safeguards |
| 9-10 | Mature, durable, observable, extensible, and proven under failure or scale |

> Can every observer make independent progress without becoming part of the execution critical path?
