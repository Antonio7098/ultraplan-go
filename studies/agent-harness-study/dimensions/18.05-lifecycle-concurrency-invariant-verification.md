# Dimension 18.05: Lifecycle and Concurrency Invariant Verification

## Purpose

Study how runtimes prove lifecycle and concurrency semantics through invariant, state-machine, race, trajectory, and leak testing.

## Steps

1. Identify explicit lifecycle and concurrency invariants.
2. Map invariants to unit, integration, property, model-based, and stress tests.
3. Inspect how tests detect illegal transitions and forbidden intermediate states.
4. Determine how terminal races and adverse interleavings are made repeatable.
5. Check whether event and state trajectories are compared as whole executions.
6. Inspect race detector, sanitizer, deadlock, and data-race coverage.
7. Identify goroutine, task, thread, subscription, and resource leak detection.
8. Check how flaky concurrency tests are isolated, repeated, and diagnosed.
9. Determine which semantic failures block CI or release.
10. Identify the evidence required before lifecycle semantics are declared stable.

## Evidence to Capture

- State-machine and transition-table tests
- Property-based and model-based test suites
- Deterministic schedulers, barriers, fake clocks, and controlled executors
- Race and stress test harnesses
- Event/state trajectory assertions
- Leak detectors and lifecycle cleanup checks
- Sanitizer and race-detector CI jobs
- Test repetition, seed capture, and failure reproduction
- Regression gates and release criteria

## Questions

1. Are lifecycle and concurrency invariants written explicitly?
2. Can tests observe illegal intermediate states, not only final results?
3. Are forbidden transitions generated systematically?
4. Can terminal races and adverse interleavings be reproduced?
5. Are complete event/state trajectories verified?
6. Are model-based or property-based techniques used where examples are insufficient?
7. How are goroutine, task, thread, observer, and resource leaks detected?
8. Do race detectors or sanitizers run in CI?
9. Are seeds and schedules preserved for failed stress tests?
10. What evidence must pass before lifecycle semantics are considered stable?

## Rating

| Score | Meaning |
|-------|---------|
| 1-3 | Absent, implicit, ad-hoc, or unsafe |
| 4-6 | Present but inconsistent, weakly documented, or fragile |
| 7-8 | Clear model with tests, explicit interfaces, and operational safeguards |
| 9-10 | Mature, durable, observable, extensible, and proven under failure or scale |

> Can the runtime demonstrate its invariants under hostile interleavings rather than merely pass happy-path tests?
